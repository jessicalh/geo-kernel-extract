# Geometric Kernel Math Lineage

Our kernel `(3 cos^2 theta - 1)/r^3` is the angular `l = 2` member of the steerable/equivariant kernel family: the same rank-2 irreducible channel used for traceless symmetric tensors, spherical harmonics, and equivariant tensor features. The confirmed outside-NMR line on disk is steerable filters, Gaussian processes on manifolds/graphs/Lie groups, and equivariant machine learning; I did not confirm a CAD-engine source in this run. The purpose of this file is to record what these mathematical characterizations license, and what they do not license, for the present application.

## What These Characteristics Let Us Say - And Not Say

- The `l = 2` channel is not the scalar channel in representation theory. Thomas et al. identify `l = 0, 1, 2` as scalar, vector, and symmetric-traceless-matrix channels (p. 4), and Geiger & Smidt identify `l = 2` with the symmetric rank-2 decomposition (p. 5). This licenses saying that our kernel uses a mathematically distinct rank-2 channel, not merely the isotropic `l = 0` channel in different notation.
- The rank-2 channel is not necessarily redundant. Ben Mahmoud et al. state that the irreducible components transform independently and can be targeted by independent models (p. 3), while Kondor 2025 states that equivariant convolution does not mix irreps (p. 6). This forbids a blanket claim that `T2` is representation-theoretically irrelevant. It does not prove that `T2` is statistically useful for our particular dataset; that remains an empirical claim.
- Equivariance gives a frame-free transformation law, not component-wise numerical invariance. Cohen & Welling 2017 give basis-independent irrep type (p. 5), Geiger & Smidt note that irreps are unique up to change of basis (p. 6), and Batzner et al. describe tensor properties under coordinate change (p. 3). This licenses saying that an equivariant `l = 2` construction is not an arbitrary chosen-local-frame artifact, provided all inputs, operations, and outputs obey the same group action.
- Completeness is conditional on the class being represented. Weiler et al. prove the generality of equivariant convolutions as equivariant linear maps between fields over `R3` (p. 1), and Batatia et al. state convergence to smooth equivariant many-body mappings under their basis assumptions (p. 5). This licenses completeness claims only inside those hypotheses: equivariant linear maps, or the stated many-body equivariant expansion. It does not license arbitrary non-equivariant or frame-breaking functions.
- For NMR tensor work, scalar-only and tensor-output targets are different modeling regimes. Bankestad et al. explicitly predict the isotropic scalar part (p. 9); Venetos et al. report that tensorial handling is key for the full shift tensor (p. 8); Ben Mahmoud et al. target irreducible tensor components (p. 3). This licenses a narrow claim: `l = 0` is adequate for isotropic-only targets, while full rank-2 tensor targets require rank-2 content.
- Manifold and Lie-group GP papers support the general point that kernels on geometric domains are constrained mathematical objects, not arbitrary similarity functions. Borovitskiy et al. require positive semidefinite kernels on manifolds (p. 1), and Azangulov et al. express stationary kernels on homogeneous spaces through irreducible representations (p. 2). This supports the "classified object" framing for geometric kernels outside NMR, but not a direct NMR theorem.
- Not settled in what I opened: no source here proves that our specific `(3 cos^2 theta - 1)/r^3` traversal improves a shielding predictor; no source here gives an if-and-only-if criterion for rank-2 redundancy in our data; no CAD/Parasolid/ACIS/CGAL source was opened that safely supports an engine-lineage claim.

## Where We Sit

We use the `l = 2` steerable kernel where these other literatures use the same irreducible harmonic/tensor channel in steerable filters, Gaussian-process kernels on geometric spaces, and equivariant machine-learning models; ours is the physics application of a known object.
