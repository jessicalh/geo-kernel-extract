## The design, distilled

- **Form.** Per-calculator "reaction-paper-style" extended README, ~2 pages, with citations.
  Not an academic submission; still a README. Under Jessica's imprint (her voice, her
  ownership). Start the same careful way as the other series.
- **Audience — one reader, three faces.** Jessica herself (bio + applied-maths undergrad +
  ~30 yr programming; can do a line integral and read Maxwell with effort; the physics is
  the gap — the literal calibration reader). The operator who skims the maths and trusts the
  shape. Her bio MSc peers, who need the physics built and the work described cleanly with
  cites.
- **The gap is physics, not maths.** Use the maths freely (line integrals, Maxwell); never
  explain it. Build the physical mechanism for someone with no physics reflexes.
- **The spine.** The physical story (what's happening), told so a non-physicist gets it; the
  maths underneath for rigor (skimmable); the cites/lineage as the grounding around it.
- **The arc.** Required-physics + the shared background (the exposition + numerics docs) →
  what's physically happening → what we measured — landed honest-small (kernels-not-shielding,
  correlate-not-match; some calculators reached a fitted law, some are still geometric signal).
- **Mechanism vs lineage.** Both; physics mechanism leads, lineage/cites ground. A reaction,
  not a literature review.
- **Calibration.** The audience is the author — Jessica's own "nope, didn't get it" on a
  draft is the exact signal.
- **The soul.** The operator who has spent a career doing other people's hard math without
  the patient's life, choosing — for her own project — to actually learn the physics.
