## 1. Classic Tensor Glyphs

**Ellipsoid glyph.** The standard second-rank tensor glyph maps eigenvectors to axes and eigenvalues to axis lengths. VTK has this baked in: `vtkTensorGlyph` "scale[s] and orient[s] glyph(s) according to eigenvalues and eigenvectors of symmetrical part of tensor" and can color by eigenvalues or scalars (https://vtk.org/doc/nightly/html/classvtkTensorGlyph.html). This is cheap and familiar, but it is weak for T2 because a traceless shielding anisotropy has mixed signs and an ellipsoid built from absolute magnitudes loses sign. TensorView makes the same NMR-specific criticism: the ellipsoid keeps PAS orientation and absolute principal-component magnitudes, but loses exact shielding-as-radius correspondence and loses sign when principal components differ in sign (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6736611/).

**Failure mode.** Kindlmann's superquadric paper gives the clean visualization critique: ellipsoids with different tensors can look similar from some views because profile and shading cues are insufficient, and cuboids overstate orientation when repeated eigenvalues make some eigenvectors underdetermined (https://people.cs.uchicago.edu/~glk/pubs/pdf/Kindlmann-Superquad1-VisSym-2004.pdf, https://diglib.eg.org/items/69471cc1-b25e-4636-bf10-02f2035257f2). This exactly matches the lead's concern: linear and planar anisotropy can read too similarly when the glyph is only a smooth ellipsoid.

**Superquadric tensor glyphs.** Kindlmann's fix is to vary the glyph between sphere, cylinder-like, and cuboid-like superquadrics so edges appear when anisotropy gives reliable orientation information, while circular cross-sections appear when repeated eigenvalues make an orientation undefined (https://people.cs.uchicago.edu/~glk/pubs/pdf/Kindlmann-Superquad1-VisSym-2004.pdf). Ennis et al. applied superquadric glyphs to diffusion and strain tensors and concluded that superquadrics clarify anisotropic/orthotropic regions better than ellipsoids (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2169197/). A later stress-tensor study reports a user-study framework and found color-encoded superquadric glyphs improved accuracy and subjective ratings for stress tensor visualization (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31985424/).

**Reynolds/Haber family.** Older mechanics/tensor-field literature includes Haber glyphs and Reynolds glyphs, especially for stress/Reynolds tensors and quadric-style tensor visualization. I found them mostly through secondary surveys and theses rather than a compact primary implementation reference during this pass (for examples: https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/6843/KratzThesis_LowRes_Online.pdf and https://www.fiberbundle.net/papers/TensorFieldViz.pdf). They are historically relevant, but I would not make them the default reader design because the NMR and e3nn sources below give a more direct match to this data.

## 2. NMR-Specific Tensor Surfaces

**Ovaloidgeometric shielding surface.** TensorView's NMR paper gives the strongest domain-specific definition: draw a surface at the atom/tensor center whose radius in a direction equals the shielding when the external field is aligned in that direction (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6736611/). TensorView calls the more exact surface an "ovaloid" and contrasts it with the popular but approximate ellipsoid (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6736611/). Its figure caption explicitly colors sign, with orange for positive shielding and light orange for negative shielding in the example (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6736611/). TensorView for MATLAB repeats the same core claim: the ovaloid is the actual shape of a symmetric second-rank Cartesian tensor because distance from center to surface is proportional to interaction strength for that field orientation (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10238149/).

For T2 specifically, the ovaloid is the rank-2 spherical signal on S^2: for a symmetric traceless tensor `S`, the directional value is `f(n)=n^T S n`; in an l=2 basis this is `sum_m a_m Y_2m(n)`. That means the NMR ovaloid and the e3nn spherical-harmonic surface are the same rendering target, just described in different communities (TensorView: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6736611/; e3nn `SphericalTensor`: https://docs.e3nn.org/en/stable/api/io/spherical_tensor.html).

**MagresView/Jmol practice.** MagresView is an NMR-crystallography tool built on Jmol, and its paper describes displaying magnetic shielding and EFG tensors as ellipsoids around atoms, with tensor parameters such as isotropy, anisotropy, asymmetry/span/skew shown as labels or color (https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/80614/, https://www.ccpnc.ac.uk/magresview/magresview/magres_view.html). This is mainstream and useful, but still mainly ellipsoid/PAS based.

**ORCA/Avogadro practice.** The ORCA 6.1 manual recommends `orca_plot` for shielding tensor visualisation and says it generates a cube file with shielding tensors depicted as ellipsoids at the nuclei, viewable in Avogadro (https://www.faccts.de/docs/orca/6.1/manual/contents/spectroscopyproperties/nmr.html). This confirms that ellipsoids remain the common computational-chemistry default, not that they are the best T2 glyph.

**SIMMOL, SIMMOLvmd, and VMD.** SIMMOL reads PDB coordinates and generates user-defined parameters for chemical shift, J, quadrupolar, and dipolar tensors, plus 3D molecular visualization with user-controlled tensor representations; it was designed to feed SIMPSON spin simulations (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11820824/). The Aarhus NMR tools page describes SIMMOLvmd as VMD scripts that specify and visualise spin systems from PDB/CIF structures and computed outputs, and describes SIMMOL as a flexible tool for visualization/definition of NMR interaction tensors (https://inano.au.dk/about/research-centers-and-projects/nmr/software-and-tools). VMD is also used as the graphical backend in the VIST shielding-tensor work (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8251739/).

**EFGShield / relative orientations.** TensorView for MATLAB discusses EFGShield as a program that reports relative orientations between chemical shielding and EFG tensors, and it documents that equivalent PAS/Euler-angle choices can look inconsistent until visualized (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10238149/). This is a reminder that axes-only tensor displays need convention handling and visible sign/orientation cues.

**Spinach.** Spinach provides `cst_display.m` and `efg_display.m`; its CST display draws chemical shielding tensors and eigensystems by scaling a unit sphere by absolute eigenvalues, so it is explicitly an ellipsoid/PAS visualization and not a signed ovaloid (https://spindynamics.org/wiki/index.php?title=Cst_display.m, https://spindynamics.org/wiki/index.php?title=Efg_display.m).

**VIST.** VIST visualizes chemical shielding tensors for aromaticity/antiaromaticity by computing a shielding tensor at points in space, diagonalising it, and rendering principal axes as local coordinate systems with VMD as a backend (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8251739/). VIST is compelling for through-space shielding fields, but for this reader's per-atom T2 it is less direct than an atom-centered surface.

## 3. ML-Era / Equivariant / Spherical-Signal Rendering

**e3nn convention.** e3nn's `SphericalTensor` is explicitly a representation of a signal on the sphere; it stores coefficients `A^l` for a function `f(x)=sum_l A^l . Y^l(x)` (https://docs.e3nn.org/en/stable/api/io/spherical_tensor.html). It also ships plotting helpers: `plot`, `plotly_surface`, `signal_on_grid`, and `signal_xyz`, and the docs use Plotly `Surface` traces for these signals (https://docs.e3nn.org/en/stable/api/io/spherical_tensor.html).

**e3nn grid path.** e3nn's S^2 grid API provides `spherical_harmonics_s2_grid`, `ToS2Grid`, and `FromS2Grid`; `ToS2Grid` transforms a spherical tensor into a signal on the sphere, and the grid stores positions on the sphere (https://docs.e3nn.org/en/stable/api/o3/o3_s2grid.html). The spherical-harmonics docs show the usual visual convention: plot scalar components on spheres, then deform the sphere so radius is the absolute value of the plotted quantity; it also derives `Y^2` as the l=2 part of `r tensor r` (https://docs.e3nn.org/en/stable/api/o3/o3_sh.html).

**Equivariant papers.** Tensor Field Networks use filters built from spherical harmonics, and each layer accepts/outputs scalars, vectors, and higher-order tensors in a geometric sense (https://research.google/pubs/tensor-field-networks-rotation-and-translation-equivariant-neural-networks-for-3d-point-clouds/, https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08219). Spherical CNNs model 3D data as multi-valued spherical functions and implement exact convolutions in the spherical-harmonic domain (https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2018/papers_ECCV/html/Carlos_Esteves_Learning_SO3_Equivariant_ECCV_2018_paper.php, https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.10130). These papers do not prescribe a chemistry glyph, but they normalize the visual language of "signal on S^2" and spherical-harmonic coefficients.

**Direct reuse.** e3nn's Python plotting helpers are not directly usable inside the C++/VTK reader, but the convention is directly reusable: evaluate five T2 coefficients on a fixed S^2 grid, displace vertices by magnitude, and attach a signed scalar array for color. That matches the local `T2[5]` storage and the e3nn coherence goal ([src/model/Types.h:508](/shared/2026Thesis/nmr-shielding/h5-reader/src/model/Types.h:508), https://docs.e3nn.org/en/stable/api/io/spherical_tensor.html).

## 5. VTK Renderability

**Ellipsoid.** Easy. Build `vtkPolyData` points at atom positions with a 9-component tensor array, feed `vtkTensorGlyph`, and use a sphere source. `vtkTensorGlyph` can use a source connection, scale by eigenvalues, extract eigenvalues, clamp scaling, and color by scalars/eigenvalues (https://vtk.org/doc/nightly/html/classvtkTensorGlyph.html). The reader already uses the same conceptual path in `TensorGlyphMath` plus `vtkSphereSource` for a selected Mat3 ([src/app/TensorGlyphMath.h:62](/shared/2026Thesis/nmr-shielding/h5-reader/src/app/TensorGlyphMath.h:62), [src/app/SceneRevealOverlay.cpp:147](/shared/2026Thesis/nmr-shielding/h5-reader/src/app/SceneRevealOverlay.cpp:147)).

**Superquadric.** Feasible but not one-line. `vtkSuperquadricSource` supports scale, theta/phi resolution, and phi/theta roundness parameters (https://vtk.org/doc/nightly/html/classvtkSuperquadricSource.html). `vtkTensorGlyph` can orient/scale a source by a tensor, but a single source has one global roundness; true Kindlmann glyphs require roundness to vary per tensor, so exact rendering needs either custom mesh generation, a source table/binning strategy, or multiple actors/glyph batches. `vtkGlyph3DMapper` does GPU glyphing like `vtkGlyph3D` and can use point attributes for orientation/scaling, but it still does not magically per-vertex-warp each glyph by five T2 coefficients (https://vtk.org/doc/nightly/html/classvtkGlyph3DMapper.html).

**Spherical-harmonic / ovaloid T2 surface.** Buildable with a fixed UV sphere grid and per-atom vertex generation: evaluate `f(n)=sum_m a_m Y_2m(n)`, place vertex at `center + scale * g(f) * n`, and color by signed `f`. `vtkSphereSource` provides a polygonal sphere and resolution control if we want a source grid, but for per-atom custom deformation a `vtkPolyData` aggregate or a `vtkProgrammableSource` is more direct (https://vtk.org/doc/nightly/html/classvtkSphereSource.html, https://vtk.org/doc/nightly/html/classvtkProgrammableSource.html). This fits the reader overlay contract because the overlay can update points/scalars in `setFrame` and let `MoleculeScene` issue one render ([src/app/MoleculeScene.h:38](/shared/2026Thesis/nmr-shielding/h5-reader/src/app/MoleculeScene.h:38), [src/app/MoleculeScene.cpp:424](/shared/2026Thesis/nmr-shielding/h5-reader/src/app/MoleculeScene.cpp:424)).

**QVTK and screenshots.** The current reader's QVTK setup matches VTK docs: `QVTKOpenGLNativeWidget` is a `QOpenGLWidget` subclass for `vtkGenericOpenGLRenderWindow`, and VTK docs require setting the default surface format before `QApplication` (https://vtk.org/doc/nightly/html/classQVTKOpenGLNativeWidget.html, [src/main_reader.cpp:69](/shared/2026Thesis/nmr-shielding/h5-reader/src/main_reader.cpp:69)). If screenshots are needed for acceptance tests, `vtkWindowToImageFilter` converts a render window into image data but requires `Modified()` after new renders to avoid stale captures (https://vtk.org/doc/nightly/html/classvtkWindowToImageFilter.html).

## Recommendation

**Recommend as primary: e3nn-native T2 spherical surface, presented in NMR language as the T2 ovaloid.** This is the best match to the thesis claim because it does not collapse T2 to `|T2|`; it renders the signed l=2 signal itself. It is also coherent with Stage 3 because e3nn's own `SphericalTensor` is a signal-on-S^2 representation with plotting and S^2-grid APIs (https://docs.e3nn.org/en/stable/api/io/spherical_tensor.html, https://docs.e3nn.org/en/stable/api/o3/o3_s2grid.html). It is also coherent with solid-state NMR practice because TensorView recommends the ovaloid as the technically correct shielding surface and explicitly colors sign (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6736611/).

Build cost: medium. It needs a new VTK overlay that aggregates per-atom meshes, a fixed real-Y2 basis adapter, a signed diverging color map, LOD/culling, and UI scale controls. It does not need eigendecomposition if fed `T2[5]`. It needs either T2 spherical components, which the reader already has, or a full symmetric/traceless `Mat3` from which T2 can be evaluated. For ORCA DFT it can use existing `SphericalTensor` fields or raw `Mat3` fields; for H5 time series it can use `(N,T,9)` or `(N,T,5)` buffers ([src/model/QtTimeSeriesBuffers.h:52](/shared/2026Thesis/nmr-shielding/h5-reader/src/model/QtTimeSeriesBuffers.h:52), [src/model/QtTimeSeriesBuffers.h:149](/shared/2026Thesis/nmr-shielding/h5-reader/src/model/QtTimeSeriesBuffers.h:149), [src/model/DftShielding.h:20](/shared/2026Thesis/nmr-shielding/h5-reader/src/model/DftShielding.h:20)).

Default behaviour: render selected atom, pinned atoms, or a local neighborhood first. For all-atom views, cull by `|T2|`, atom selection, residue/element filters, and screen-space budget. Use color for sign, not radius sign alone: shape should show magnitude/lobe geometry while a diverging palette distinguishes shielding versus deshielding. Provide a sign-convention toggle or legend because chemical shielding sigma and chemical shift delta are opposite conventions in NMR practice (TensorView's shift conversion discussion: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6736611/).

**Recommend as secondary overview mode: superquadric PAS glyph.** This is the best "classic but perceptually stronger than ellipsoid" option. It conveys linear versus planar anisotropy better than ellipsoids and has some perceptual/user-study support in tensor visualization (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2169197/, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31985424/). Use it when many atoms must be shown cheaply and exact NMR ovaloid semantics are less important. Data needed: full symmetric/traceless `Mat3` or reconstructed `Mat3` from T2, plus eigendecomposition and linear/planar/spherical shape metrics. Build cost: medium-low for binned/source-table approximation, medium-high for exact per-tensor Kindlmann roundness.

**Do not make the plain ellipsoid the default.** Keep it as a debug/reference mode because it is cheap and VTK-native, but it is exactly the representation with the known ambiguity and sign-loss failure modes (https://people.cs.uchicago.edu/~glk/pubs/pdf/Kindlmann-Superquad1-VisSym-2004.pdf, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6736611/).

Bottom line: build the **T2 spherical surface / ovaloid** first for correctness and e3nn coherence, then optionally add **superquadrics** as a dense-scene overview mode. This gives the lead one scientifically exact modern view and one proven at-scale glyph view, without reducing T2 to a scalar.
